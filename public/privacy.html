Privacy

Last updated 17 August 2026

The short version. We have no accounts and no advertising, and we do not track you. During the free week nothing you record leaves your device at all. Once you have a licence key, your progress is carried between your own devices — filed under a scrambled fingerprint of that key, with no name and no email attached, so the record cannot say who anybody is.

What is stored, and where

Everything the app knows lives in your browser first, and the app works with no connection at all:

  • The nickname you give each reciter
  • Which books you have marked, their status, the dates and your favourite surahs
  • Your ayah notes and which days you practiced
  • Your licence key, and whether the free week has started

How your progress reaches your other devices

A family uses a tablet, a phone and a laptop, so progress has to travel. Here is exactly how.

Your licence key is put through a one-way scramble — a fingerprint. The same key always produces the same 64-character string, and there is no way back from that string to your key. That fingerprint is the only label your progress is filed under. It is not your email, not your name, not your order number, and it cannot be turned into any of them.

Filed under it are the things in the list above, minus the licence key itself: the nicknames, the books and their status, the dates, the ayah notes and the practice days. Never your key, your email, your name, your address, your age or your payment details.

Two things follow from this that are worth saying plainly:

  • During the free week there is no key, so there is no fingerprint and nothing is stored anywhere but your own browser. The trial is entirely private
  • Removing a reciter removes them everywhere — from that device, from your other devices the next time they connect, and from our records

The store is Supabase, a database service, in the United States. We can see the rows in it; they say nothing about who you are. If you would like your family's records deleted outright, write to us and we will remove them — and you will still have your books on your own devices.

You can also keep a copy yourself with the Save a copy of your progress button, and load it back at any time. That is worth doing before you clear a browser during the free week, when there is no other copy.

What else leaves your device

When you unlock the app, and about once a week afterwards, your licence key is sent to Lemon Squeezy to check it is still valid. Nothing travels with it — no name, no email, no progress, no reciters. If that check cannot be made, the app carries on as normal.

If you subscribe, Lemon Squeezy collects your payment and billing details directly. They are the merchant of record and their privacy policy covers that information. We can see order records in their dashboard; we never see your card details.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which like any web host records requests to its servers.

Cookies

We set none, and there are no tracking scripts, pixels or advertising networks here. Nothing to consent to, and no banner to dismiss.

Everyone in the household

The app is for anyone memorizing the Quran, at any age, and families often use it together. It asks for nothing but a nickname, and a nickname filed under a fingerprint is not something we can trace to a person. Please use a nickname rather than a full name — that is the whole reason the app never asks for one.

Your rights

We hold very little, and none of it is tied to your identity. Even so: write to us and we will delete your family's records from the sync store outright, or tell you what is in them. Clearing the app's data on a device removes that device's copy. For anything Lemon Squeezy holds about a purchase, contact them or write to us and we will help.
