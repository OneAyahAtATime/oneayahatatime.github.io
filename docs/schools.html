Schools, circles & masjids

Last updated 20 August 2026

Memorizing the Quran is rarely a solitary thing. A weekend school class, a masjid youth halaqa, a small group of friends holding each other to a surah a week — One Ayah At A Time was built around one family, but the same shelf works just as well for a class or a circle.

We are a small, Muslim-owned family business. This started as something we built for our own household, and hearing from teachers and organizers has been one of the nicest surprises since. If you are bringing this to a class, a study circle, or a masjid program, we would love to hear from you — write to us and we will work with you directly.

How it works for a group today

Every student, or every circle member, gets their own shelf — their own books, their own dates, their own certificates — the same way a family adds a reciter for each child. Nobody's progress is tangled up with anybody else's, and nothing is ranked or compared. A teacher can set up a shared device for a class, or point families to install it at home and bring their own progress in; either way, the pace stays personal.

What we do not have yet

We want to be honest about this rather than let a page oversell it: there is no teacher dashboard, no assignment list, and no way to see a whole class's progress at a glance. What exists today is the same journey a family uses, used by more people. If a class needs its own arrangement — more than one household on a single license, for instance — get in touch and we will figure it out together rather than make you guess at our pricing page.

What guides anything we build for classrooms

If we ever do build teacher-facing features, these are the lines we will not cross:

  • No rankings. A teacher might see that a class is practicing, never a leaderboard of who is ahead
  • No one else's notes. A student's private reflections stay theirs, not visible to a teacher or classmates
  • No red flags. Muraja’ah and a pause in practice are a normal part of the journey, not something that shows up as a warning to an adult in charge
  • Nothing about a child's Quran journey travels further than it has to — the same care that keeps the app free of names and emails today would extend to any class feature

Gentle support for a class should never become surveillance or comparison. We would rather move slowly on this and get it right than ship something that turns memorization into a scoreboard.

Try it with your class or circle

The same seven free days apply — no card, no account. Set it up on however many devices your group needs and see whether it fits before anyone commits to anything.
